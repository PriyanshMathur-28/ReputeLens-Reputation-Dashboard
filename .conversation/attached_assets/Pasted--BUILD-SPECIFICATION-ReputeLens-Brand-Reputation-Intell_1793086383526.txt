## 1. PROBLEM STATEMENT

Brands are discussed across thousands of social posts, comments, reviews and news articles daily. PR and marketing teams cannot read everything, so they miss:

- WHAT people feel (sentiment and emotion) and ABOUT WHICH part of the business (price, quality, delivery, support, etc.)
- WHO is driving the conversation (influencers, amplifiers, bridges between communities, coordinated bot-like accounts)
- WHEN ordinary complaints are turning into a crisis
- WHY it is happening (the underlying narratives)
- WHAT to do about it, and whether the response worked

ReputeLens solves this with a 3-layer intelligence pipeline that feeds one dashboard:

1. **NLP layer**: cleaning, language detection, hybrid sentiment, aspect-based sentiment, emotion, sarcasm, complaint/intent/toxicity detection, Hinglish support, narrative (topic) discovery, keyword trends, risk keywords, fake-review signals.
2. **Social Network Analytics (SNA) layer**: author interaction graph, PageRank/betweenness influence, Louvain communities, coordinated inauthentic behaviour detection, sentiment by community, information-spread simulation.
3. **LLM layer**: conversational Q&A over the data with citations (RAG), spike explanations, crisis response copilot, executive reports, competitor summaries.

Outputs are combined into an explainable **Reputation Health Score (0–100)** and a **Crisis Risk (0–100)** with a 7-day forecast and an alert engine.

### Target users
- Brand/PR manager: wants health score, alerts, what to do.
- Social media analyst: wants mentions, narratives, influencers, network.
- Executive: wants a weekly brief.

**Pipeline (`pipeline.py`)** for each RawMention:
1. Deduplicate by (source, external_id) and by `text_hash` (sha1 of normalized text) within the same author.

6. Run NLP analysis (Section 9).
7. Commit in batches of 200.
8. After the batch: trigger SNA recompute, topic recompute (if ≥ 50 new mentions), daily metrics, alerts.

## 9. NLP LAYER (`backend/app/nlp/`)

### 9.1 F1 – Hybrid sentiment (`sentiment.py`)
- Always compute VADER compound on `clean_text`.
- For Hinglish/Hindi text, first apply the Hinglish lexicon (Section 9.5) to compute a lexicon score; offline score = average of VADER and lexicon score.
- If LLM available: LLM returns `sentiment_score` in −1..1 (prompt P1, Section 14.1).
- Final score:
  - Both available: `0.4 * vader + 0.6 * llm`
  - If `is_sarcastic` is true and LLM available: use LLM score alone.
  - Offline only: VADER (or VADER+lexicon average for Hinglish).
- Label: score > 0.05 → positive; score < −0.05 → negative; else neutral.
- Offline sarcasm heuristic (sets `is_sarcastic`): positive VADER (> 0.3) AND contains a negative aspect cue (late, cold, stale, missing, wrong, 90 minutes, hours, again) OR patterns like "wow", "great", "truly", "love it" followed by a complaint word within the same sentence. When triggered offline, flip the sign of the score and multiply by 0.7.

### 9.2 F2 – Aspect-Based Sentiment (`absa.py`)
Fixed taxonomy: `delivery`, `food_quality`, `price`, `customer_support`, `app_experience`, `refunds`, `hygiene`, `packaging`, `other`.
- Offline: `lexicons/aspects.json` maps each aspect to 20–40 English + Hinglish keywords (e.g., delivery: late, delivery, rider, delivered, delay, "der se", "late aaya"...). Split text into sentences; for each sentence containing an aspect keyword, aspect sentiment = VADER (+Hinglish lexicon) of that sentence; evidence = that sentence (trimmed to 120 chars).
- LLM: prompt P1 returns aspects with sentiment, score and an evidence span copied verbatim from the text.
- Output stored as list: `[{"aspect": "delivery", "sentiment": "negative", "score": -0.7, "evidence": "arrived 90 minutes late"}]`.

### 9.3 F3 – Emotion (`emotion.py`)
8 classes: joy, anger, fear, sadness, surprise, disgust, trust, neutral.
- Offline: `lexicons/emotions.json` (NRC-style, ~60 words per emotion incl. Hinglish and emoji tokens). emotion_scores = normalized keyword counts; top emotion if max count > 0 else neutral.
- LLM: prompt P1 returns emotion and per-emotion probabilities.

### 9.4 F4 – Flags (`flags.py`)
- `is_complaint`: negative sentiment AND (aspect present OR complaint cues: "worst", "never again", "refund", "complaint", "not received", "nahi mila").
- `is_purchase_intent`: cues "should I order", "planning to buy", "worth it?", "subscribe", "which is better", "kya lena chahiye".
- `is_question`: ends with "?" or starts with question words.
- `toxicity` (0–1): offline = share of tokens in `lexicons/toxic.json` (profanity/insults list incl. common Hindi slurs), scaled and capped at 1; LLM overrides when available.

### 9.5 F5 – Multilingual
- `langdetect` with seed fixed (`DetectorFactory.seed = 0`).
- Devanagari characters present → `hi`.
- Latin script AND ≥ 2 tokens found in `lexicons/hinglish_vocab.json` (common Hinglish words: hai, nahi, bahut, tha, kya, mast, bekar, ekdum, accha, bura, khana, paisa, wapas...) → `hinglish`.
- `lexicons/hinglish_sentiment.json`: ~300 Hinglish/Hindi words and phrases with polarity −1..1 (bekar −0.7, mast 0.8, ghatiya −0.9, zabardast 0.9, "paisa vasool" 0.8, "time waste" −0.6...).
- LLM handles all languages natively and also returns an English translation (stored in explanation context, shown in the mention drawer).

### 9.6 F6 – Narrative discovery (`topics.py`)
Runs per brand over the selected window (default last 60 days, max 5,000 most recent mentions).
1. Vectors: LLM embeddings if available (cached in `embedding`); otherwise TF-IDF (1–2 grams, max_features 5,000, English stopwords + custom Hinglish stopwords) reduced with TruncatedSVD to 100 dims.
2. KMeans for k in 4..12, pick k with best silhouette score (sample 2,000 points for silhouette to keep it fast).
3. Keywords per topic: top 8 TF-IDF terms by cluster centroid (class-based TF-IDF).
4. Label & summary: LLM prompt P2 (3–6 word label + 2-sentence summary). Offline: label = top 3 keywords title-cased joined by " · "; summary = template using size, avg sentiment and top aspect.
5. Per topic store mention_count, avg_sentiment, negative_share, first_seen, last_seen, daily_counts.
6. **Emerging** if: count in last 48h ≥ 3 × (7-day average daily count × 2) AND ≥ 20 mentions in last 48h. growth_rate = (last 48h count) / max(1, previous 48h count) − 1.
7. Since the "today" of demo data is after the crisis decays, emerging detection must ALSO be evaluated historically for every day (sliding window) and stored on the Topic as `emerged_on` date in daily_counts metadata, so the UI can show "emerged on day 45". Add field `emerged_on` (date nullable) to Topic.

### 9.7 F7 – Keyword & hashtag trends (`keywords.py`)
- Top 50 terms (excluding brand keywords and stopwords) and top 30 hashtags for a period, with counts, avg sentiment, and change vs previous equal-length period.
- "Rising terms": terms with largest positive change in frequency (min count 10).
- Word cloud data: `[{text, value, sentiment}]` — UI colors words by sentiment.

### 9.8 F8 – Risk keyword watchlist (`risk.py`)
- Match brand `risk_keywords` (word-boundary, case-insensitive; plus Hinglish variants) and store matches in `risk_flags`.
- Categories for display: hygiene/safety (plastic, hair in food, cockroach, food poisoning, unsafe, expired, hospital), legal (lawsuit, consumer court, fssai, legal notice), trust (scam, fraud, data leak), activism (boycott).
- Feeds alert type `risk_keyword` and priority score.

### 9.9 F9 – Suspicious/fake review detection (`fake_reviews.py`)
For mentions with source review_csv, compute `fake_review_score` (0–1) = mean of these signals (each 0 or 1, or 0–1):
- Rating–text mismatch: rating ≥ 4 with sentiment < −0.2, or rating ≤ 2 with sentiment > 0.4.
- Near-duplicate: TF-IDF cosine > 0.85 with another review from a different author within 7 days.
- New account: account_age_days < 14.
- Burst: author is among ≥ 10 same-rating reviews for the same brand within 48h.
- Generic text: < 6 words and no aspect detected.
Flag as suspicious if score ≥ 0.6. Show counts per brand on the Competitors page and in the Inbox filter.

### 9.10 Priority score (F25)
For each mention:
```
severity  = max(0, -sentiment_score)                              # 0..1
reach     = log10(1 + reach_estimate) / log10(1 + max_reach_in_brand)   # 0..1
influence = author.influence_score / 100                          # 0..1
risk      = 1 if risk_flags else 0
priority_score = 100 * (0.35*severity + 0.25*reach + 0.25*influence + 0.15*risk)
```
`reach_estimate` = followers × 0.1 + likes + 3×shares + 2×replies (compute at ingestion).

### 9.11 Explanation
Every analyzed mention stores a one-line explanation, e.g. "Negative (−0.72): complaint about hygiene ('plastic in biryani') and support; emotion disgust; risk keyword: plastic." Built from the analysis fields offline; LLM-provided when available.

## 10. SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYTICS LAYER (`backend/app/sna/`)

### 10.1 Graph build (`graph_builder.py`)
- Per brand, directed weighted `networkx.DiGraph`. Nodes = authors who mentioned the brand in the window. Edge u→v for each Interaction (u replied to / mentioned / shared / quoted v). Weight = sum of interaction weights (reply 1.0, mention 0.8, share 1.2, quote 1.0).
- Node attributes: followers, avg_sentiment of their brand mentions, mention_count.
- Cache the computed graph metrics in Author rows; recompute after ingestion or on demand.

### 10.2 F10 – Influence (`centrality.py`)
- PageRank (alpha 0.85, weight="weight").
- Betweenness centrality: exact if n ≤ 500, else approximate with `k=min(200, n)`, `seed=42`.
- In/out degree (weighted).
- Influence score:
```
raw = 0.40*minmax(pagerank) + 0.30*minmax(log1p(followers)) + 0.20*minmax(in_degree) + 0.10*minmax(avg_engagement)
influence_score = 100 * raw
```
`avg_engagement` = mean(likes + shares + replies) of the author's mentions.

### 10.3 F11 – Communities (`communities.py`)
- Louvain on undirected projection: `networkx.community.louvain_communities(G_undirected, weight="weight", seed=42)`.
- Per community: size, avg sentiment, negative share, dominant topic (most common topic_id), dominant aspect, top 3 influencers, auto-name (offline: "Community #N · <dominant topic label>"; LLM: short descriptive name via prompt P6).

### 10.4 F13 – Bridges
Top 2% by betweenness who connect ≥ 2 communities (have edges into at least 2 different community_ids) → role `bridge`.

### 10.5 F12 – Coordinated inauthentic behaviour (`bot_detection.py`)
Per author signals (each 0–1):
- `s_new` = 1 if account_age_days < 30 else 0
- `s_low` = 1 if followers < 100 else 0
- `s_burst` = 1 if ≥ 5 posts within any 60-minute window, scaled
- `s_dup` = fraction of the author's posts with TF-IDF cosine > 0.85 to posts by OTHER authors within ±30 minutes
- `s_ratio` = 1 if following/followers > 10 else 0
```
bot_likelihood = 0.20*s_new + 0.15*s_low + 0.20*s_burst + 0.35*s_dup + 0.10*s_ratio
```
Cluster detection: build a similarity graph connecting authors whose posts are near-duplicates within 30 minutes; connected components with ≥ 5 authors and mean bot_likelihood ≥ 0.5 = **coordinated cluster**. Store cluster id, member ids, first/last post time, shared hashtags, sample texts, and which influencer they amplified (most common reply/share target). Authors with bot_likelihood ≥ 0.7 get role `suspected_bot`.

### 10.6 Roles
Priority order: suspected_bot → influencer (top 2% influence_score, min 3 authors) → bridge → amplifier (out_degree in top 10% AND ≥ 60% of their posts are shares/replies) → regular.

### 10.7 F14 – Spread simulation (`cascade.py`)
Independent Cascade model:
- Activation probability on edge u→v (information flows from v's post to u who interacts — use the REVERSED graph so influence flows from the target to the interacting followers): `p = min(0.3, 0.05 + 0.25 * normalized_weight)`.
- Inputs: seed author ids (or "top 5 negative influencers"), steps (default 10), runs (default 200, max 500), scenario.
- Scenarios: `no_response` (base p), `fast_response` (p × 0.6 from step 2 onward), `influencer_outreach` (removes the top 3 bridge nodes from activation).
- Output per scenario: expected activated count per step (mean and 10th/90th percentile), expected total reach (sum of followers × 0.1 of activated nodes), list of the 50 most-frequently activated node ids.
- Must finish in < 5 seconds for 1,000 nodes (use adjacency lists and numpy random, not networkx calls inside the loop).

## 11. METRICS & SCORES (`backend/app/services/metrics.py`)

### 11.1 Daily metrics (per brand per day)
- volume, positive, neutral, negative counts
- `net_sentiment = (positive − negative) / max(1, volume) × 100`  (range −100..100)
- `avg_sentiment` = mean sentiment_score
- `share_of_voice = brand_volume / (brand_volume + sum competitor volumes)` for the same day
- engagement = sum(likes + shares + replies); reach = sum(reach_estimate)
- `influencer_sentiment` = mean sentiment of mentions by authors with role influencer (null → 0)

### 11.2 F15 – Reputation Health Score (0–100)
Compute for each day using a trailing 7-day window ending that day:
```
C1 net sentiment       = (net_sentiment_7d + 100) / 2                       weight 0.35
C2 sentiment trend     = clip(50 + 500 * slope_of_avg_sentiment_7d, 0, 100) weight 0.15   (slope via numpy.polyfit, per day)
C3 crisis safety       = 100 − crisis_risk                                   weight 0.20
C4 influencer sentiment= (influencer_sentiment_7d + 1) * 50                  weight 0.15
C5 share of voice      = clip(100 * sov_7d / fair_share, 0, 100)            weight 0.15   (fair_share = 1 / number_of_brands_compared)
health_score = round(Σ weight × component, 1)
```
Store each component in `health_components` so the UI can show the exact breakdown and which component moved most.

### 11.3 F16 – Crisis Risk (0–100) and forecast
```
neg_series = daily negative counts
mean14, std14 = trailing 14-day mean and std (excluding today); std floor = max(std14, 1)
z = (today_neg − mean14) / std14
C_spike       = clip(20 × z, 0, 50)
C_narrative   = 15 if any emerging topic with negative_share ≥ 0.6 in last 48h
C_influencer  = 15 if any influencer mention with sentiment ≤ −0.5 in last 24h
C_coordinated = 10 if a coordinated cluster active in last 48h
C_risk_kw     = 10 if ≥ 10 risk-keyword mentions in last 24h
crisis_risk = min(100, sum of components)
```
Risk bands: 0–24 Low (green), 25–49 Elevated (yellow), 50–74 High (orange), 75–100 Critical (red).

Forecast: Holt's linear exponential smoothing implemented in numpy (alpha 0.5, beta 0.3) on the last 30 days of negative counts, 7 days ahead, with 95% band = forecast ± 1.96 × std(residuals). Return `{date, forecast, lower, upper}`. Show on Overview and Crisis Center.

### 11.4 F24 – Geography
Per city: volume, net sentiment, top negative aspect, change vs previous period. Mentions without a city are grouped as "Unknown" and excluded from ranking.

### 11.5 F23 – Response impact
For each ResponseAction at time T:
- before window = T−72h..T, after window = T..T+72h (or until now)
- report: avg sentiment before/after, negative volume per day before/after, % change, mentions acknowledging the response (offline: cues "responded", "apolog", "refund received", "they replied", "jawab diya"; LLM: classify), and a verdict: Effective (sentiment ↑ ≥ 0.1 and negative volume ↓ ≥ 20%), Partially effective, Not effective.

## 12. F17 – ALERT ENGINE (`backend/app/services/alerts.py`)

Runs after each metrics recompute; also backfilled across the whole demo period at seed time so historical alerts exist (with their original dates).

| Type | Trigger | Severity |
|------|---------|----------|
| negative_spike | negative-count z ≥ 2.5 | z ≥ 5 critical, ≥ 3.5 high, else medium |
| volume_spike | total volume z ≥ 3 | z ≥ 5 high, else medium |
| emerging_narrative | topic becomes emerging | negative_share ≥ 0.6 → high, else medium |
| influencer_negative | influencer mention sentiment ≤ −0.5 | followers ≥ 100k high, else medium |
| coordinated_activity | coordinated cluster detected | ≥ 20 accounts high, else medium |
| score_drop | health score down ≥ 10 points vs 1 day earlier | ≥ 20 critical, else high |
| risk_keyword | ≥ 10 risk-keyword mentions in 24h | hygiene/legal categories → high, else medium |

Rules: no duplicate open alert of the same type for the same brand within 24h (update the existing one instead). Each alert stores evidence (top 10 mention ids by priority score), related topic and related authors. Status workflow: open → acknowledged → resolved, editable in UI.

## 13. LLM SERVICE (`backend/app/services/llm.py`)

```python
class LLMClient:
    def is_available(self) -> bool
    def provider_name(self) -> str          # "openai" | "gemini" | "replit" | "offline"
    def complete(self, system: str, user: str, json_schema: dict | None = None, max_tokens: int = 1200) -> str
    def complete_json(self, system: str, user: str, model_cls: type[BaseModel]) -> BaseModel | None
    def embed(self, texts: list[str]) -> list[list[float]] | None
```
- Provider order: Replit AI Integrations → OPENAI_API_KEY → GEMINI_API_KEY → offline. `LLM_PROVIDER` overrides.
- temperature 0.2 for analysis, 0.5 for drafting.
- `complete_json` validates with pydantic; on failure retries once with "Return ONLY valid JSON matching the schema"; returns None on second failure (caller falls back).
- Timeout 30s; on HTTP 429/5xx retry with exponential backoff (1s, 2s, 4s), then fall back.
- Cache all results in LLMCache by sha256(task + model + input).
- Embeddings in batches of 100; truncate texts to 2,000 chars.
- Expose `GET /api/system/status` returning provider, model, connectors configured, mention counts, last ingestion time.

## 14. LLM FEATURES & PROMPTS (`backend/app/intel/`, prompts in `prompts.py`)

### 14.1 P1 – Batch mention analysis (used by F1–F5)
System:
"You are an expert brand-reputation analyst for the Indian market. You understand English, Hindi and Hinglish, Indian slang, and sarcasm. Analyze each social media mention about the brand. Be precise and conservative. Copy evidence spans verbatim from the text."
User: brand name, aspect taxonomy, emotion list, and a JSON array of up to 20 `{id, text}`.
Required JSON output:
```json
{"results": [{"id": 123, "sentiment_score": -0.8, "is_sarcastic": false,
  "emotion": "anger", "emotion_scores": {"anger": 0.7, "disgust": 0.2, "neutral": 0.1},
  "aspects": [{"aspect": "hygiene", "sentiment": "negative", "score": -0.9, "evidence": "found plastic in my biryani"}],
  "is_complaint": true, "is_purchase_intent": false, "toxicity": 0.1,
  "english_translation": null, "explanation": "Strong hygiene complaint with disgust."}]}
```

### 14.2 P2 – Topic labelling (F6)
Input: top keywords + 15 representative mentions (closest to centroid). Output: `{"label": "3-6 words", "summary": "2 sentences", "sentiment_reason": "1 sentence"}`.

### 14.3 F19 – "Ask ReputeLens" RAG chat (`rag.py`)
1. Parse the question for brand, time range, aspect, competitor, sentiment (use LLM function-style JSON extraction when available; offline regex/keyword matching on aspect names, competitor names, "last week", "yesterday", "day 45", dates).
2. Retrieve: top 30 mentions by cosine similarity (embeddings or TF-IDF) filtered by the parsed constraints, re-ranked by 0.7 × similarity + 0.3 × priority_score/100.
3. Attach a "facts pack" of computed aggregates for the range: volume, net sentiment, health score change, top aspects, top topics, top influencers, alerts.
4. LLM answer (prompt P3) with rules: "Use ONLY the facts pack and mentions. Cite mentions as [M<id>]. If the data does not support an answer, say so. Never invent numbers."
5. Response: `{answer_markdown, citations: [mention objects], facts_used, suggested_followups: [3 questions]}`.
6. Offline: templated answer composed from the facts pack + top 5 retrieved mentions, clearly labelled.
7. Suggested starter questions in UI: "Why did sentiment drop around the crisis?", "What are customers' biggest complaints about delivery?", "Which influencers are most negative this month?", "How does our refund sentiment compare to QuickBite?", "Did our official response work?", "What are Hinglish-speaking users saying?"
8. Multi-turn: pass last 6 messages as history.

### 14.4 F20 – Explain this spike (`explain.py`)
Input: brand, date. Gather that day's metrics vs 14-day baseline, top topics that day, top 20 mentions by priority, top authors, alerts. LLM prompt P4 → `{headline, what_happened, main_drivers: [..], who_drove_it: [..], is_organic: "organic|coordinated|mixed", recommended_next_step}`. Offline: template from the same data (is_organic = coordinated if a coordinated cluster was active that day).

### 14.5 F21 – Crisis Response Copilot (`crisis.py`)
Input: alert_id or topic_id. Gather evidence mentions, aspects, emotions, communities involved, influencers and bridges involved, coordinated cluster info, risk keywords, spread simulation results (no_response vs fast_response).
LLM prompt P5 output:
```json
{"root_cause": "...", "evidence_summary": ["..."],
 "severity_assessment": "...",
 "strategy": "acknowledge|clarify|apologize_and_fix|monitor|counter_misinformation",
 "strategy_reasoning": "...",
 "draft_replies": [{"tone": "empathetic", "text": "..."}, {"tone": "formal", "text": "..."}, {"tone": "brief", "text": "..."}],
 "public_statement": "...",
 "outreach_list": [{"author_id": 1, "handle": "@x", "reason": "..."}],
 "do_not": ["..."],
 "monitoring_plan": "..."}
```
Outreach list comes from SNA (top influencers and bridges in affected communities, excluding suspected bots). If a coordinated cluster is involved, the strategy must mention it and advise reporting the accounts rather than engaging them. Offline: rule-based strategy (hygiene/legal risk → apologize_and_fix; coordinated & low organic share → counter_misinformation; otherwise acknowledge) and template drafts filled with brand name, aspect and city. Always show a banner: "AI-generated drafts — review before publishing." Button "Log as response" creates a ResponseAction.

### 14.6 F22 – Executive Brief (`briefs.py`)
Input: brand, period (default last 7 days, compared with previous 7). Markdown sections:
1. Headline (one sentence)
2. Health Score: value, change, biggest moving component
3. Key numbers table (volume, net sentiment, share of voice, crisis risk) with change
4. Top 3 positive drivers (with 1 quoted example each, short)
5. Top 3 negative drivers (with examples)
6. Emerging narratives
7. Network insights: top influencers, notable communities, coordinated activity
8. Competitor comparison
9. Response effectiveness (F23)
10. Recommended actions (3–5, prioritized)
Offline: same structure filled from templates. UI renders Markdown, offers "Download .md" and "Download PDF" (print stylesheet + `window.print()`).

### 14.7 F18 – Competitor summary
LLM prompt P7 over aspect-level net sentiment of all brands → "Where we win / Where we lose / Opportunities" (3 bullets each). Offline: computed by comparing aspect net sentiment differences (> 10 points).

### 14.8 P6 – Community naming
Input: community's top keywords, dominant topic, sample 10 mentions → 2–4 word name.

## 21. CODING STANDARDS & ERROR HANDLING

- Python: type hints everywhere, pydantic schemas for all I/O, `logging` (not print), no bare `except:`.
- TypeScript: strict mode, shared types in `types.ts`, no `any` unless unavoidable.
- Wrap every external call (LLM, HTTP connectors) in try/except with logging and a graceful fallback.
- Never hardcode secrets; read from environment only. Never log secrets.
- Database indexes as specified; use bulk inserts for seeding.
- Long tasks run as background jobs with progress reporting.
- Frontend: every data component handles loading, empty, and error states; API client has a global error interceptor that shows a toast.
- Use UTC in the database and display in the browser's local time zone.
- Keep functions small and modules focused; no file over ~400 lines.
- All demo data is fictional.
